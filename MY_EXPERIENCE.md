# My AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner Online Exam Experience

This is a personal account of taking the **AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)** exam through online proctoring. It is intended to help other candidates prepare for the practical side of exam day.

It does **not** contain live exam questions, reconstructed exam content, or private appointment details. Exam rules can change, so always treat your AWS/Pearson VUE confirmation email and current official policies as the source of truth.

## Start check-in early

I began the online check-in process about **25 minutes before my scheduled exam time**. That was useful because the verification process took several steps before I was allowed to begin.

My advice is simple: do not plan to click the exam link at the exact appointment time. Be fully ready beforehand and use the early check-in window available for your appointment.

## Have your ID ready

Bring a valid government-issued ID that meets the requirements in your appointment instructions.

A **passport** is a straightforward option if it is accepted for your booking and region. Make sure:

- the ID is original and valid
- the name matches the name on the exam booking
- the photo and details are clear
- you do not wait until check-in to discover an ID mismatch

Never publish photographs, numbers, barcodes, candidate IDs, registration IDs, or other identifying details from your exam documents.

## Prepare the room before check-in

The room setup matters almost as much as the laptop setup.

For my session:

- nobody else was in the room
- I removed my watch
- my desk was cleared
- my phone was used only where needed for the verification stage and then kept away
- the room had enough lighting for my face and surroundings to be clearly visible

I would recommend preparing the room before launching check-in rather than trying to clean things up while the proctor is waiting.

### A useful room checklist

- Private room with no other person present
- Door closed and interruptions prevented
- Clear desk and surrounding workspace
- No notes, books, paper, or study material within reach
- No watch or smartwatch
- No headphones or earbuds unless your specific instructions explicitly allow them
- One active display unless your current rules state otherwise
- Good lighting on your face and room
- Phone placed away after the permitted verification stage

## Expect a room inspection

At the start of my session, the proctor contacted me by video and asked me to show the room. I had to move my **laptop camera around the room** so the proctor could inspect the testing environment.

Be prepared to show more than just your desk. Keep the whole room exam-ready.

If you are using a laptop, make sure it is easy enough to move briefly during inspection without disconnecting power or network equipment.

## Test the exact setup you will use

Before the exam, test the same:

- computer
- webcam
- microphone
- speakers
- internet connection
- room/location

Close unnecessary applications, disable VPN software, and restart the computer early enough that updates or background processes do not surprise you during check-in.

Keep the laptop connected to power.

## Strong, stable internet matters

A stable connection is extremely important for an online-proctored exam because the proctor needs a continuous video and audio feed.

In my case, the exam was temporarily **paused** because the proctor messaged me that my webcam feed was no longer visible on their side. I could not immediately tell that anything was wrong from my side.

The check took roughly **3–4 minutes**, after which I was able to resume the exam.

The lesson for me was:

> If something technical happens, do not panic or start changing things randomly. Follow the proctor's instructions, stay available in the testing interface, and let the proctor verify the setup before continuing.

A short interruption does not necessarily mean the session is over.

## Keep your phone away after verification

Your phone may be useful during the permitted check-in flow, depending on the current process. Once that stage is complete, put it **out of reach and out of sight** according to the instructions you are given.

Do not keep checking notifications, touching the phone, or leaving it beside the keyboard.

## Stay visibly inside the testing environment

Once the exam begins:

- stay in camera view
- avoid repeatedly looking away from the screen
- do not read questions aloud
- do not use notes or another device
- do not let anyone enter the room
- contact the proctor through the approved interface if there is a problem

Your session is being proctored, so make your behavior easy to interpret.

## The questions can be deceptively simple

Cloud Practitioner is foundational, but some questions are still tricky because several answers may look reasonable.

Pay close attention to words such as:

- **BEST**
- **MOST cost-effective**
- **fully managed**
- **without interruption**
- **least operational overhead**
- **over the internet**
- **dedicated connection**
- **high availability**
- **shared file system**

Often, the difference between two plausible answers is hidden in one requirement.

Do not answer only from service-name recognition. Ask:

1. What is the actual requirement?
2. Which answer satisfies **all** of it?
3. Which options solve a related problem but miss one key condition?

## Use the review time

I finished the first pass with time remaining, so I reviewed my choices **two more times**.

That was useful because a second reading can reveal words you missed the first time.

If you have extra time:

- revisit flagged questions
- re-read multiple-response questions carefully
- check whether you selected the requested number of answers
- look again at questions where two options seemed close
- avoid changing a correct answer unless you can explain why the new choice is better

Do not race to submit simply because you finished early.

## My practical exam-day checklist

Before check-in:

- Valid government-issued ID, such as an accepted passport
- Laptop and charger
- System test completed on the intended setup
- Webcam, microphone, and speakers tested
- Strong and stable internet connection
- VPN and unnecessary background apps closed
- Clean, private, well-lit room
- Nobody else in the room
- No watch or smartwatch
- No notes or study material nearby
- Phone ready only if needed for check-in, then put away
- Bathroom and other needs handled before the session

During the exam:

- Stay calm if the proctor pauses the session
- Read qualifiers carefully
- Eliminate answers that fail even one requirement
- Flag uncertain questions and return to them
- Use remaining time to review before submitting

## One final reminder

Do not use this or any community experience report as a substitute for your current appointment instructions.

Online-proctoring requirements, accepted IDs, software requirements, check-in procedures, and exam policies can change. Review the current official AWS Certification and Pearson VUE information before your own exam.

And on the exam itself: some questions will make you pause, but if you understand the core AWS concepts and read the wording carefully, **you've got this**.
