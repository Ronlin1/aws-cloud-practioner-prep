# My AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner Online Exam Experience

This is a personal account of taking the **AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)** exam through Pearson VUE OnVUE online proctoring. It is intended to help other candidates prepare for the practical side of exam day.

It does **not** contain live exam questions, reconstructed exam content, or private appointment details. Exam rules can change, so always treat your AWS/Pearson VUE confirmation email and the current official policies as the source of truth.

**Last policy check:** 2026-09-26

Official AWS OnVUE page:
https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/aws/onvue.html

## Start check-in early

I began the online check-in process about **25 minutes before my scheduled exam time**.

Pearson's current AWS OnVUE guidance says candidates should **begin check-in 30 minutes before the appointment**. My advice is therefore simple: be completely ready before the early check-in window opens.

Do not use the appointment time as the moment you begin preparing your room or looking for your ID.

## Have your ID ready

Bring a valid government-issued photo ID that meets the requirements in your appointment instructions.

Pearson currently lists an **international passport** as an accepted ID type. The name on the ID must exactly match the name on the exam booking.

Make sure:

- the ID is original and valid
- the name matches the exam booking
- the photo and details are clear
- you do not wait until check-in to discover an ID mismatch

Never publish photographs, numbers, barcodes, candidate IDs, registration IDs, or other identifying details from your exam documents.

## Prepare the room before check-in

The room setup matters almost as much as the laptop setup.

For my session:

- nobody else was in the room
- I removed my watch
- my desk was cleared
- my phone was used only where needed for the verification stage and then kept away
- the room had enough lighting for my face and surroundings to be clearly visible

I recommend preparing the room before launching check-in rather than trying to clean things up while the proctor is waiting.

### A useful room checklist

- Private, quiet room with no other person present
- Door closed and interruptions prevented
- Clear desk and surrounding workspace
- No notes, books, paper, pens, or study material within reach
- No watch or smartwatch
- No headphones or earbuds
- **One display only**
- Good lighting on your face and room
- Phone placed away after the permitted verification stage
- No one else able to view your screen

Pearson currently prohibits public testing spaces such as libraries and coffee shops for OnVUE.

## Expect a room inspection

At the start of my session, the proctor contacted me by video and asked me to show the room. I had to move my **laptop camera around the room** so the proctor could inspect the testing environment.

Pearson's current flow includes a **360-degree room scan** during check-in.

Be prepared to show more than just your desk. Keep the whole room exam-ready.

If you are using a laptop, make sure it is easy enough to move briefly during inspection without disconnecting power or network equipment.

## Test the exact setup you will use

Before the exam, test the same:

- computer
- webcam
- microphone
- speakers
- internet connection
- room/location

Pearson currently says to run and pass the **system test on the same device and network** you plan to use on exam day.

Also:

- restart the computer before check-in
- close unnecessary applications
- disable VPN software
- keep the laptop connected to power
- disconnect or cover additional displays as required

## Internet requirements matter

A stable connection is extremely important because the proctor needs continuous video and audio, not merely enough bandwidth to load exam questions.

Pearson currently states a minimum of:

- **6 Mbps download**
- **2 Mbps upload**

It also currently prohibits:

- VPNs
- corporate networks
- public/shared networks

and recommends making sure nobody else on the network is doing heavy streaming or large downloads during the exam.

Use the strongest and most stable compliant connection available to you and test that exact network in advance.

## My webcam feed disappeared for the proctor

This was the most unexpected part of my session.

Partway through the exam, the proctor messaged me that my webcam feed was no longer visible on their side. From my side, it was not immediately obvious that anything had failed.

My **testing session was temporarily interrupted while the issue was checked**. After roughly **3–4 minutes**, I was able to resume.

The important policy nuance is that Pearson's current public guidance says an in-exam proctor **cannot formally pause or extend the exam timer**. So I describe what happened to me as a testing-session interruption rather than assuming that the timer itself was officially paused.

The lesson for me was:

> If something technical happens, do not panic or start changing things randomly. Follow the proctor's instructions, stay available in the testing interface, and let the approved process guide the next step.

Pearson says to use the in-exam chat to reach a proctor. If the computer freezes or disconnects, follow the current relaunch instructions shown by Pearson for your testing program.

## Keep your phone away after verification

Your phone may be used during the permitted check-in flow depending on the current process. Once that stage is complete, put it **out of reach and out of sight** according to the instructions you are given.

Pearson's current rule is straightforward: do not access your phone during the exam unless a proctor explicitly permits it.

Do not keep checking notifications, touching the phone, or leaving it beside the keyboard.

## Stay visibly inside the testing environment

Once the exam begins:

- stay in camera view
- avoid repeatedly looking away from the screen
- do not read questions aloud
- do not use notes or another device
- do not let anyone enter the room
- do not allow anyone else to view your screen
- contact the proctor through the approved interface if there is a problem

Your session is proctored and recorded, so make your behavior easy to interpret.

## The questions can be deceptively simple

Cloud Practitioner is foundational, but some questions are still tricky because several answers may look reasonable.

Pay close attention to words such as:

- **BEST**
- **MOST cost-effective**
- **fully managed**
- **without interruption**
- **least operational overhead**
- **over the internet**
- **dedicated connection**
- **high availability**
- **shared file system**

Often, the difference between two plausible answers is hidden in one requirement.

Do not answer only from service-name recognition. Ask:

1. What is the actual requirement?
2. Which answer satisfies **all** of it?
3. Which options solve a related problem but miss one key condition?

## Use the review time

I finished the first pass with time remaining, so I reviewed my choices **two more times**.

That was useful because a second reading can reveal words you missed the first time.

If you have extra time:

- revisit flagged questions
- re-read multiple-response questions carefully
- check whether you selected the requested number of answers
- look again at questions where two options seemed close
- avoid changing a correct answer unless you can explain why the new choice is better

Do not race to submit simply because you finished early.

## My practical exam-day checklist

Before check-in:

- Valid government-issued photo ID such as an accepted passport
- Booking name matches the ID exactly
- Laptop and charger
- System test completed on the **same device and network**
- Webcam, microphone, and speakers tested
- One display only
- At least 6 Mbps download / 2 Mbps upload on a stable connection
- No VPN, corporate network, or public/shared network
- Unnecessary background apps closed
- Clean, private, quiet, well-lit room
- Nobody else in the room
- No watch or smartwatch
- No headphones or earbuds
- No notes, paper, pens, or study material nearby
- Phone ready only if needed for check-in, then put away
- Bathroom and other needs handled before the session
- Begin check-in about 30 minutes before the appointment

During the exam:

- Follow all on-screen and proctor instructions
- Stay in camera view
- Do not read questions aloud
- Do not access your phone unless explicitly permitted
- Read qualifiers carefully
- Eliminate answers that fail even one requirement
- Flag uncertain questions and return to them
- Use remaining time to review before submitting

## One final reminder

Do not use this or any community experience report as a substitute for your current appointment instructions.

Online-proctoring requirements, accepted IDs, software requirements, check-in procedures, networking rules, and exam policies can change. Review the current official AWS Certification and Pearson VUE information before your own exam.

And on the exam itself: some questions will make you pause, but if you understand the core AWS concepts and read the wording carefully, **you've got this**.
